# Sync Strategies

## Grass Sync (DataManager — uploads/approvals/notes)
- **Incremental**: Start from last cached date minus 3-day safety buffer
- **Integrity check**: For past years, compare remote count vs local sum; force re-fetch on mismatch
- **Completion cache**: Mark past years as complete to skip future fetches
- **Unused caches dropped after 90 days (T-606, decision G-6)**: opening a profile records `sync_meta.lastVisitedAt` (`noteGrassVisit`, numeric ids only), and 10 s later, once a day (`di.grassCache.sweptAt`, the tag pages' `claimDailySweep`), `sweepGrassCache` empties the six Grass tables (`uploads`, `approvals`, `approvals_detail`, `notes`, `completed_years`, `hourly_stats`, as `clearCache` does) of every other user last opened more than 90 days ago; their past years are fetched again on the next visit. A user cached before visits were recorded gets one at the first sweep, which starts their 90 days; a user known only by name is left alone. The user on screen is never swept, so it never meets the integrity check's year deletion
- **Days and hours in one zone (T-702, N-08)**: Danbooru writes `created_at` in the viewer's account time zone (`<body data-current-user-time-zone>`; a logged-out viewer gets Danbooru's default, `Eastern Time (US & Canada)`). A day cell is the timestamp's first ten characters, which is also the zone a cell's `date:` search is read in, and since T-702 the Hourly Distribution's hour is the two digits after the `T` (`timestampHour`), not `Date#getHours` on the browser's clock. The two differ only for a viewer whose browser zone is not the account's: a logged-out viewer outside US Eastern now sees the hours in US Eastern time, as the day cells already were. Years cached before T-702 keep the hours they were counted with (`hourly_stats` has no basis mark) until the year is fetched again — a reset, a 90-day sweep (T-606) or an integrity mismatch. The user dashboard's monthly chart places its milestone stars the same way, by `created_at`'s first seven characters, as its bars count
- **Batch**: 200 items/page. Page 1 alone, then — only when it came back full — 5 pages at a time (3 for a delta fetch) (T-604; a full fetch used to ask 5 at once, 4 empty answers for a year under 200 uploads)
- **Page retries as the full sync's (T-603)**: `fetchPageWithRetry` uses the full sync's `classifySyncFetchError` / `syncRetryDelayMs` (`core/fetch-retry.ts`): a 429, a 5xx, a network failure or an unreadable body is retried up to `SYNC_PAGE_MAX_ATTEMPTS` (5) times, each wait covering what is left of the shared limiter's pause; another 4xx fails at once. Before, four 429s in a row (1, 2 and 4 s apart, shorter than a long pause) failed the year, and a network error failed it without a retry
- **Uploads from the synced posts (T-601, audit DA-25)**: a year with nothing cached in `uploads` is filled from the user's `posts` rows (`fillUploadsYearFromPosts`) when they can stand for it (`syncedPostsUsable`): a completion stamp (`danbooru_grass_last_sync_*`), no `syncStartedAt` or `needsFullSync`, `rowNumberingIntact`, and `sync_meta.timeZone` equal to the page's `<body data-current-user-time-zone>` — `created_at` carries that zone, and the day is its first ten characters and the hour the next two after the `T`, as with the API's items. Rows are read by `[uploader_id+created_at]` for [Jan 1, next Jan 1), the range `date:` asks for. Then the usual path: a past year's integrity count checks the sum (a match marks it complete with no page request; a mismatch deletes the year and pages it); the current year fetches from its last day − 3, as for any cached year. Any failed condition or error leaves the year to the API. Approvals and notes are untouched. A cached past year also no longer sends the empty `<next Jan 1>...<next Jan 1>` page request

## User Analytics Sync (AnalyticsDataManager)
- **Quick Sync** (`quickSyncAllPosts`): For users with ≤1200 total posts. Sequential cursor-based pagination. Auto-triggered in `renderDashboard()`.
- **Full Sync** (`syncAllPosts`): For large users. Standard pagination with retry, streaming iteration for memory efficiency.
- **Full Sync pace (v9.12)**: `SYNC_WORKERS` (5) workers, each cycling through one page's server time (about 1 s live) plus a 400 ms gap, so a lone sync averages about 3.5 req/s: the worker count sets the pace. The shared limiter's cap (4.5 req/s, T-213) only trims the moments when pages come back faster — and those bursts are what Cloudflare refuses (about 50 requests per 10 s; see CLAUDE.md "Rate Limiting"). Measured on a 46k-upload account, 2026-11-13: 5 workers under a 6 req/s cap drew one or two 429s in every full sync; 4 workers under 6 req/s still drew one (82 s); 4 workers under 4.5 req/s drew none in three runs (100–131 s); 5 workers under 4.5 req/s drew none in three more (102–161 s, that evening, when the busiest minute held only 128–163 requests against 176–182 in the morning with 4 workers). So the server's time per page moves with its load (about 1 s to about 2 s) and decides the run time more than either number here; compare timings only between runs made back to back. 4 workers (T-211) was a detour from reading the 429s as a two-minute budget, since ruled out. After changing either number, check `Sync traffic` (`edge429s`, `elapsedMs`) and an expanded `429 received` entry (`sent10s`).
- **Fields version 2 (T-402)**: both syncs ask for `SYNC_POST_FIELDS` (`core/post-record.ts`) — the old fields plus `updated_at`, `is_pending`, `is_flagged`, `tag_count`, `tag_string_character`, `tag_string_copyright`; `variants` is no longer asked (a post search never returned it), and since T-506 neither is `preview_file_url` (about 100 bytes a row; the milestone lookup fetches the few thumbnails shown, with `media_asset[variants]`, and keeps them on those rows until a sync rewrites them) — and build every row with `toPostRecord`. A run that wrote all of the user's rows (Full sync from `startId` 0 that completed, or a Quick sync) then sets `sync_meta` `fieldsVersion: 2`, `changeWatermark` = the server's `Date` on its first page (the rows reflect Danbooru as of then at the latest; the client clock is only a fallback) and `timeZone` = `<body data-current-user-time-zone>`. A resume sync re-fetches only the last month and leaves all three alone, so older rows without the fields do not pass for version 2; T-403 backfills them.
- **Fields backfill (T-403)**: `needsPostMetadataBackfill` is true for a user with rows whose `sync_meta.fieldsVersion` is below 2 (outside the failure cooldown); the old `di_post_metadata_v2_*` flag is no longer read or written (version 2 includes that metadata). `backfillPostMetadata` fetches **every** row the user had at its start — not just the rows that lack the fields: the watermark it records is the server time of its first response, so every row must be fetched after it (a row a resume sync wrote earlier would otherwise miss the changes in between) — in `BACKFILL_RANGES` (3) id ranges walked in parallel (`user:X status:any id:a..b order:id`, 2 terms), rebuilding each with `toPostRecord` under its own `no`. The plan and each range's progress live in `sync_meta.backfill`, saved after every page, so an interrupted run resumes with the same watermark; rows a sync adds meanwhile are outside the ranges and already version 2. At the end: `fieldsVersion 2`, `changeWatermark`, `timeZone`, plan cleared. Failures follow the old cooldown (3 failed runs → 24 h, `di_backfill_failure_<id>` in localStorage; a run counts once however many of its ranges fail; a 429 only stops the run). The failure warnings carry the page URL. The scatter widget runs it and shows "updating… %"; an account without a scatter (under 300 uploads, or no points) runs it unseen after paint. `clearUserData` deletes the user's `sync_meta` row with the rows.
- **Page-limit segments (v9.12, T-212)**: Danbooru answers 410 past the viewer's numbered-page limit (`User.page_limit`: 1000 below Gold, 5000 from Gold; `PaginationExtension`). The sync's search is `user:X order:id id:>N`, so it runs in segments of `SYNC_SEGMENT_PAGES` (1000, fits every level): when a segment's pages all came back with posts, the next one sets `N` to the last post committed and counts pages from 1 again. Before, a Member's sync of an account above 200,000 uploads stopped at page 1001 as incomplete and needed a "Resume Sync" per 200k. Ghost pruning and the completion stamps still use the run's first `startId`.
- **From the first post, not id 0 (T-508)**: `user:X order:id id:>0` makes Danbooru walk the primary key from id 0 until the user's posts begin. For an account whose uploads start millions of ids in, the first pages took 7–8 s logged in and failed the 3 s logged-out limit with a 500 (browser check 2026-11-16, `user:Alixiron`: 3.4 s and `ActiveRecord::QueryCanceled`, against 0.35 s by date and 0.66 s by `id:>` the first post). A sync from the start (Full sync `startId` 0, and Quick sync's cursor) now begins at `findFirstPostAnchor`: `user:X order:id date:>=<the day before the join date>&limit=1&only=id`, trusted only when `/counts/posts.json` `user:X id:<first` is 0 (the profile scrape falls back to "now" for a join date it cannot read, which would otherwise skip the older posts). Any failure or doubt starts at `id:>0` as before. Two more requests (one a count) per sync from the start; `startId` stays 0 for the ghost prune and the completion stamps.
- **End and back-pressure of the page pool (T-504)**: a page shorter than the page size (200) is the last: it is committed and no worker claims another (pages already in flight past it come back empty). Before, the run only ended on an empty page, so every sync asked for a few pages past the end. Pages commit in order, so a page stuck in its retries used to let the other workers pile pages up behind it without bound; now a worker claims a new page only while the reorder buffer holds fewer than `SYNC_BUFFER_MAX_PAGES` (25, 5,000 posts), and otherwise looks again every 250 ms. `syncAllPosts`' `options.pageLimit` exists for tests that serve smaller pages.
- **Why Full Sync is not a cursor walk (measured 2026-11-13, T-210)**: a sequential id-cursor version (`user:X`, `page=a<last id>`, one page at a time) was built and run on a 46k-upload account: 235 pages took **285 s**, about 1 request per second, with no edge 429. Danbooru spends roughly 1 s on a 200-post page whichever way it is paged (the 5 workers on numbered pages also see about 1 s each), so the cursor's cheaper seek buys nothing and the lost parallelism costs about 3×. A cursor needs the previous page's last id, so it only parallelises by splitting the id range into segments. Do not retry the plain sequential cursor.
- **Page retries (v9.10)**: `classifySyncFetchError` sorts a failed page fetch into `rate-limit` (429), `transient` (5xx, the 30 s timeout abort, network `TypeError`, unparseable body) or `fatal` (other 4xx). The first two retry up to `SYNC_PAGE_MAX_ATTEMPTS` (5); `syncRetryDelayMs` waits out the shared rate limiter's remaining pause (`backoffRemainingMs()`) before each retry, so the request's 30 s timeout is not spent in a paused queue. Before v9.10 only 5xx retried, and one 429 stopped a 46k-post sync around page 210.
- **Pies from the local rows (T-406, decision G-2)**: `getLocalDistributions(uploaderId)` counts the user's rows in one scan (`createLocalDistributionCounter`) when all hold: `sync_meta.fieldsVersion ≥ 2`, `syncStartedAt` unset and a completion stamp (`danbooru_grass_last_sync_*`), `rowNumberingIntact`, at least one row (a debug log `Pies count remotely {reason}` says which failed). Then `getStatusDistribution` sends only `status:appealed` (an appeal is not a post field, N-07), `getRatingDistribution` and `getUserStats` send nothing, and `getCharacterDistribution` / `getCopyrightDistribution` (`localTagDistribution`) skip `/related_tag.json` and the per-candidate counts: tags ranked by exact local count, the top-level check (`getTopLevelFlags`, 50 names a lookup, at most 200) walking that order until ten survive, sub-tag breakdowns from local counts (`attachSubTagBreakdownsFrom`), Others = rows − Σ top ten. Shapes and piestats keys are unchanged; thumbnails still come from `enrichThumbnails`; Fav-copyright still counts. The counts come from the dashboard's one scan of the rows (T-501: `getRowSnapshot`, shared with the summary stats, the scatter and the monthly chart, kept 60 s on the open's manager — one dashboard load creates one; before, each of the four read every row), and the scan and the pies are dropped after a sync, a backfill, a catch-up that changed rows or a reset (`forgetLocalRows`). The catch-up runs on the open's manager for that reason. A 46k-upload account's cold open saves about 70–120 counts (status 5, rating 4, user stats 2, character 35 + subs, copyright 22 + subs). The values are those of the last sync or catch-up (T-407), not live counts.
- **Change catch-up (T-407, decision G-2)**: after the dashboard paints, `scheduleChangeCatchUp` runs `catchUpChanges`: `user:X order:change` (newest `updated_at` first, 2 terms), 200 a page with `SYNC_POST_FIELDS`, overwriting the rows it has under their own `no` (posts the rows lack are the sync's). It walks until a row older than `sync_meta.changeWatermark` (equal does not stop it); the new watermark is the newest `updated_at` of its first page, never lower than the old. Usually one request. It runs only for rows of fields version 2 with a watermark, outside a sync, and once per count TTL (`caughtUpAt`, `catchUpWatermark`). Progress `{page, headAt}` is saved in `sync_meta.catchUp` after every tail page; a resumed run first reads from page 1 down to the saved `headAt` (posts changed since moved to the top), then goes back to one page before the saved one (unread posts can only have moved down; an expunged post can pull them up by one) — and saves nothing during that head phase, so an interruption there keeps the older progress (`core/change-catch-up.ts`, pure). Past the page limit (`SYNC_SEGMENT_PAGES`) it sets `needsFullSync`: the local pies count remotely and the next sync starts from 0; a full sync or a finished backfill clears it, and the catch-up progress with it. When rows changed and the pies are local, status / rating / character / copyright are recounted and live-patched (`DanbooruInsights:DataUpdated`). Score changes do not touch `updated_at`; the resume sync's last-month re-fetch still carries them.
- **Row numbering check and the sync mark (T-404, audit DA-07)**: `rowNumberingIntact` compares the user's row count with the highest `no` (index reads only: a count on `uploader_id`, the last key of `[uploader_id+no]`). A gap means rows went missing below the newest (a ghost prune, an old bug); a resume only re-fetches the last month and could never fill it, so "Data Synchronization Required" kept coming back. `calculateResumeStartId` now starts such a user from 0. Both syncs set `sync_meta.syncStartedAt` when they start and clear it when they complete (`recordFullSyncMeta`, or the resume branch of `finalizeSyncMetadata`); a failed run keeps it. `cleanupStaleData` (via `isStaleUser`) keeps a user whose sync started within `ANALYTICS_CLEANUP_THRESHOLD_MS`, stamped or not — before, a first sync that failed had no completion stamp, so the next cleanup (after any other user's sync) deleted everything it had committed.
- **Other users' cleanup after paint (T-503)**: `cleanupStaleData` no longer runs inside either sync, where it held the dashboard back while it deleted; the app runs it once the dashboard after a sync is painted (`scheduleStaleCleanup`, when `syncJustRan` was set). A sync whose dashboard is not drawn (the resume view) leaves it to a later one. A stale user's rows are deleted by their index keys in one `bulkDelete` (a collection delete walked a cursor over every row), with its piestats, `sync_meta` and `user_stats` rows (T-505; the quota eviction and `clearUserData` drop the last two as well), and the run ends by pruning expired `tag_implications_cache` rows.
- **Resume gate (v9.10)**: `renderDashboard` decides "Data Synchronization Required" from the pre-check (`needsResumeSync`) before `fetchDashboardData`; only the Quick Sync path keeps the post-fetch check, since its pre-check numbers are stale.
- Both strategies store posts in IndexedDB and call `refreshCriticalStats()` to populate the piestats cache. Heavy tag distributions are deferred and freshen post-paint via `fetchDashboardData`'s SWR revalidate (v9.8.5).
- **Sync outcome (v9.8.7)**: `syncAllPosts` returns `SyncOutcome {complete}`. A worker that exhausts its retries stops quietly, so the run resolves normally with only some pages committed; `complete: false` marks that. `finalizeSyncMetadata` then withholds the last-sync stamp and the backfill-complete flag (the stats refresh still runs), and the app-layer callers show a warn toast. Callers must not paint a success state on `complete: false`.
- **Overlap reconcile (v9.8.7)**: a post that has vanished from Danbooru's search entirely — expunged (permanently destroyed), or no longer credited to this uploader — never appears in the re-fetched window. *Deleted* posts are not in this group: Danbooru's default search includes them (verified 2026-10-29 with the sync's own `user:X order:id id:>N` query — no `status:` metatag — which returns `is_deleted: true` rows), so the re-fetch simply overwrites their row with the deleted flag. (The original v9.8.7 note claimed deleted posts were excluded; that premise was wrong, the prune itself is still correct.) After a **clean** run that committed at least one page, `pruneGhostPosts(uploaderId, startId, seenIds)` deletes this user's rows above `startId` that the remote did not return. Without it the stale row survives with `is_deleted:false` while survivors are renumbered, so its old `no` collides with a live post's (ambiguous `[uploader_id+no]` milestone lookups, local count drifting above remote). It runs *after* the fetch, not as a delete-then-refetch before it, so an interrupted sync can never leave a hole; a partial run is skipped because an unseen id may just be a page we never asked for. Quick Sync is unaffected — it clears the user first.
- **Quota-safe writes (v9.4)**: bulk inserts on `posts` go through `bulkPutSafe()` from [src/core/quota-manager.ts](../../src/core/quota-manager.ts). On `QuotaExceededError` (or `AbortError` whose `.inner` resolves to one), `evictOldestNonCurrentUser(db, currentUserId)` deletes the least-recently-synced non-current user's posts/piestats and the bulk write retries once. The current user's data is never evicted. Writes inside `db.transaction(...)` keep the raw `bulkPut` (e.g. Grass uploads/approvals/notes) — wrapping there would risk `PrematureCommitError`.
- **Persistent storage opt-in (v9.4)**: After the first successful Quick/Full sync completion, `requestPersistence()` calls `navigator.storage.persist()` (idempotent via the `di.persist.requested` localStorage flag). Lifts Safari ITP eviction and Chrome heuristic eviction risk for users who actually engaged with the dashboard.

## Grass first on a profile page (v9.12, decision G-7)
Grass and the report share one rate limiter (4.5 req/s), so a report sync started while Grass is still fetching slows both. `GrassLoadGate` ([src/core/grass-load-gate.ts](../../src/core/grass-load-gate.ts)) is on while the *current* Grass view loads: `updateView` turns it on with the spinner and off in `finally` only if it is still the current run (a superseded run keeps filling the cache without holding the button). While it is on, a 📊 click shows a toast and starts neither the sync nor the dashboard; past 400 ms of loading the button also shows as waiting (`di-grass-locked`, `aria-disabled`). The hover preview, the ≤`MAX_PREVIEW_ONLY_UPLOADS` pinned-preview click and anything already running are not held. The reverse (changing the Grass year while a sync runs) still overlaps.

## Tag Analytics Sync (TagAnalyticsApp)
- **Cache-First**: Load from `tag_analytics` table, check 24h expiry + post count diff
- **Delta Sync**: if the post count grew by ≥ threshold (50), re-run the analysis reusing the cached first posts, first-100 rankings and milestones (only the missing milestone targets are fetched)
- **Full Sync**: For new tags, fetch all posts and aggregate by user
- **Small Tag Optimization**: Tags with ≤1200 posts (`MAX_OPTIMIZED_POSTS`) are fetched entirely into memory — history, rankings, and milestones calculated locally without DB storage.
- **Monthly history (v9.12, T-301–T-303)**: always fetched whole by `fetchMonthlyHistory`, in UTC months, on a revisit too. (1) one `/reports/posts.json` `period=month` request for 2005 to today; (2) on a 5xx (Danbooru's statement timeout) the range is halved at a month boundary and asked again, down to 12-month pieces and 24 requests, summed month by month, and only then are the closed months cached in `tag_monthly_counts` so the next run asks only for what follows them; (3) otherwise one `/counts/posts.json` per month with explicit UTC bounds (`date:YYYY-MM-01T00:00:00Z...`). A lost month in (3) makes the history `partial`: shown with a warn toast, the report is not saved. Pure helpers: `core/monthly-report.ts`. Through v9.11 the history was one count per month in the viewer's time zone, merged incrementally with the cached history, plus a backward month-by-month scan when the counts fell short of the total.
- **Layer 3 stays (T-310, decided 2026-11-14)**: in browser checks touhou (1.1M posts) and idolmaster (290k) finished in layer 1 logged in (4 runs), and touhou in layer 2 logged out (9 requests, 4 of them timed out at Danbooru's 3-second logged-out limit, 25 s, nothing partial). Layer 3 was never used, but it is the only way to a history when the reports fail outright (an outage, a spent reports lane, a limit change), so it stays, with its month-row TTL, drift check and 90-day rescan (`lastFullScanAt`). Look again if a `Monthly history {layer: 3}` log shows up, or if layer 3 gets in the way of another change.
- **Report rankings Danbooru did not return**: a logged-out viewer's all-time rankings of a tag as large as touhou time out (3 s) every time; reports cannot be split and summed for a top 10. A failed slot with no cached list is saved in `rankingsFailed` and its column says so — "Danbooru timed out on this ranking: logged-out searches stop after 3 seconds. Log in to see it." for a logged-out viewer (`isAnonymousViewer`), "…could not return this ranking. It is fetched again on the next analysis." otherwise — instead of "No Data", which reads as "nobody". `cachedRankingResult` carries the saved failures, so `keepCachedOnFailure` never treats a failed (empty) cached list as a ranking.
- **Rating and status counts (T-306)**: ratings come from one `search[group]=rating` report (from the history's first month), deleted and active from one `search[group]=is_deleted` report — active = not deleted − pending − flagged, Danbooru's own `Post.active` (deleting a post clears pending and flagged, and a pending post cannot be flagged). Pending, flagged, appealed and banned stay counts. A failed report falls back to counting (4 ratings; deleted + active). 10 counts became 2 reports + 4 counts.
- **Related-tag pies (T-307)**: the Copy / Char pies of a large tag start as `/related_tag.json` approximations (frequency × post count, no counts). The exact values — one `/counts/posts.json` per slice, up to 10 — are fetched the first time the user opens that tab (`_refineRelatedCountsOnce` → `fetchExactRelatedCounts`), drawn in place, and kept on the cached report (`patchCachedReport`, without changing its age) with `relatedCountsExact`, so neither a second open nor a later cache hit counts again. A run with a failed count is not marked and retries on the next open. A small tag's pies are counted from its posts and marked exact. Through v9.11 the exact counts were fetched in the background on every analysis, whether or not the tab was ever opened.
- **Milestones (T-304)**: `milestoneLookup` finds the month whose running total reaches the target, then the post at offset `k` in it with `<tag> status:any date:>=YYYY-MM-01T00:00:00Z order:id`, `limit = min(200, ceil(k / 1000))`, `page = ceil(k / limit)`, row `(k − 1) % limit` — the page stays ≤ 1000, every level's page limit, up to 200,000 posts into the month (past that the milestone is skipped). Three lookups at a time. Through v9.11 it searched `date:>` the previous month's last day (a bare date in the viewer's time zone, which let that day's posts in, so the post found was early by them) with `limit=200`, one lookup after another.
- **Older posts than the tag (rename case)**: `fetchInitialStats` looks from the tag's creation date. When the history's first month is earlier than the first post found, `_refreshFirstPostsIfOlder` fetches the first posts again and recomputes the first post, the 100th post and the first-100 rankings. The search is the history's narrow UTC range holding the first 200 posts (`firstPostsDateTerm`: `date:<first month>T00:00:00Z...<month after the running total reaches 200>T00:00:00Z`), retried once when it comes back empty. The earlier open `date:>=<first month>` (a bare date, read in the viewer's time zone) matched all 1.1M touhou posts and answered 500 in a browser check (2026-11-14), which left the later first posts in place until the next analysis.
- **Dates (T-305 follow-up)**: every date on the tag dashboard is a UTC day (`utcDayOf`): the header's Created / Updated, the status label by the button, the latest post, the milestone cards and the star tooltips. Danbooru's `created_at` carries the viewer's account time zone and the browser's calendar may differ from both; near midnight the same post read as different days in different places.
- **The chart follows the same months (T-305)**: a click on a month opens `<tag> date:YYYY-MM-01T00:00:00Z...<next>T00:00:00Z` (`monthPostsQuery`) — a bare date range would be read in the viewer's Danbooru time zone and open a different month than the bar counts. Year ticks, milestone stars (`utcMonthStartOf`) and the cumulative tooltip use the `YYYY-MM-01` keys, never the browser's calendar. The monthly chart title carries "UTC" and the tag dashboard footer a line saying so. The link keeps Danbooru's default search (no `status:any`), so a month with deleted posts can show fewer posts than its bar.
- **Lost counts (T-308)**: a status, rating, commentary or translation count that could not be had is `null` in its pie (`TagCountMap`), shown as unknown ("—", or "🔒" over the viewer's tag limit — see api-endpoints.md "Search tag limit"), never 0; a slice derived from it (active, Untagged) is unknown too. `saveToCache` keeps the saved report's pie when that one is whole and the new one is not (`keepKnownCounts`, pie by pie), so a count Danbooru did not return never replaces one it did; the open dashboard still shows the run's own. Without a whole previous pie the `null`s are saved. The status / rating refresh on a cache hit (`_refreshCountOverlays`) keeps a whole cached pie the same way, and every path stamps `countsUpdatedAt` only when status and rating both came back whole, so the earlier stamp stays and a later open asks again. Through v9.11 every failure read as 0 and was saved for a day.
- **Cache cleanup (T-309)**: `cleanupOldCache` (on every analysis and after a retention change) deletes reports older than the retention period (`danbooru_tag_analytics_retention`, 7 days) together with their tags' `tag_monthly_counts` rows, in one transaction. Once a day (`di.tagCache.sweptAt` in localStorage, claimed before it runs) it also sweeps month rows without `basis: 'utc'` and month rows no report owns that were fetched before the retention period — a first visit writes its months before saving its report, and a partial history caches them without one, so recent rows stay — and prunes `tag_implications_cache` past its TTL (`pruneImplicationCache`). The sweep runs from tag pages; the user dashboard writes implication rows too, and since T-505 its stale-user cleanup after a sync prunes them as well. Through v9.11 only the reports were ever deleted.
- **UTC mark**: a `tag_analytics` record carries `historyBasis: 'utc'`, a `tag_monthly_counts` row `basis: 'utc'`. `loadFromCache` does not use a record without it (the analysis runs again, and the tag's month rows are dropped); month rows without it are not read.
